Trainline ran the Pay As You Go (PAYG) trial for East Midlands Railway (EMR), which introduced a new problem: unlike Trainline's normal flow of billing before travel, PAYG users travelled first and were billed afterwards based on the journey they'd actually taken. This meant the systems that supplied UK fare data — traditionally built to answer "what does this journey cost right now" — needed to also answer "what did this journey cost N days ago," since we were the only team in the business that needed to look backwards rather than at current fares.

I took ownership of this fare retrieval problem: understanding how the industry fare data was structured and consumed across Trainline's legacy systems, and designing an approach to retain and serve a rolling window of historical fare data so the PAYG flow could always charge the correct fare for a journey after the fact. This meant extending systems that had never needed to preserve historical state in this way, while keeping current-fare lookups working exactly as before for the rest of the business. It was a cross-functional effort under a fixed deadline (the PAYG trial start date), delivered on time.

On top of this, due to the time constraints of the trial, a partial solution was implemented that would suffice for the initial months of the trial, due to the initial static nature of the fares. I also designed a longer-term solution designed to scale beyond the trial and oversaw a thorough handover of this project (in the form of detailed written documentation as well as in depth pairing sessions) to ensure understanding was not lost when I left Trainline.
